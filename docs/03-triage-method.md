# Step 3: Triage method

Work through all 30 emails in this order. Record every decision in the `emails` sheet (or Gmail labels).

## 3.1 Read the whole inbox first (15 minutes)

1. Skim all subjects to spot clusters: the same client appears in several emails, and some emails duplicate each other.
2. Note today's date: **Wed, Oct 7, 2026**. Anything with a deadline before that is already late.
3. Check `Sent` and `Drafts` as well as `Inbox`. Sent emails create follow-ups, and drafts are unfinished work.

## 3.2 Ask five questions per email

| # | Question | Where to record it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is it real, or spam/phishing? | Spam folder or `label` |
| 2 | What does it ask for, or what has changed? | `action` |
| 3 | Who must act: Sarah or the VA? | `owner` |
| 4 | When is it due, and when should I check back? | `deadline`, `followup_date` |
| 5 | Is a reply needed, and does Sarah need to approve? | `response_needed`, `approval_needed` |

## 3.3 Choose a priority

| Priority | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|
| Urgent | Upset client, money at risk, or due today |
| High | Due within 3 days, contracts, overdue invoices, price conflicts |
| Medium | Due within 2 weeks, routine replies, scheduling |
| Low | Newsletters, promotions, receipts, spam |

## 3.4 Choose a label

| If the email... | Use |
|---|---|
| needs someone to do or decide something | 01 - Action Required |
| holds facts about a client (preferences, referrals, context) | 02 - Client Information |
| is about an invoice, payment, billing or renewal | 03 - Invoices & Payments |
| needs a nudge on a future date | 04 - Follow-Up |
| involves a meeting, invite or reschedule | 05 - Meetings & Calendar |
| is something you sent and are waiting on | 06 - Waiting for Client |
| is finished | 07 - Completed |
| is useful later but needs no action | 08 - Reference |

Gmail lets one email carry **several** labels. If an email is both an invoice and an action, use both, and choose a
"primary" one for your tracker. Keep a note on why.

## 3.5 Flag problems (this is the real skill)

Start a `Issues Log` tab with columns: `issue_id`, `type`, `where_found`, `description`, `question_for_Sarah`, `status`.

Look for:
- **Conflicts:** two prices, two dates, "paid" vs "overdue".
- **Duplicates:** the same request twice, repeated tasks, two client records for one person.
- **Missing information:** a promised attachment, a blank due date, a draft with `[add date]`.
- **Schedule clashes:** two meetings at the same time, a time with no time zone.
- **Inconsistent names:** "Tom" vs "Thomas", "Greenleaf Landscaping" vs "Greenleaf Landscape".
- **Safety:** phishing signs (urgent threat, odd domain, asks you to log in).

## 3.6 Decide what you can do alone

- **VA can handle:** logging payments, resending invoices, adding calendar events, chasing documents, drafting replies.
- **Needs Sarah:** price changes, discounts, contracts, disputed invoices, cancelling subscriptions, deciding between meetings.

When unsure, **draft** the reply and ask Sarah to approve. Don't send it.

## 3.7 Write a draft message to Sarah

After triage, write one summary email listing the 5 most urgent items, the decisions you need from her, and the problems you found.
Keep it under 200 words. Example subject: `Inbox review: 5 items need your decision by Thursday`.

## 3.8 Compare with the answer key

Only now open `answer_key/`. For each difference, write one line about your reasoning. Differences with good reasons are not mistakes.
